In December 2022, with ChatGPT's afterglow still in the air, a search product called Perplexity launched. It did not mimic the "ten blue links" shape of traditional search engines. Instead it built an "answer engine": users ask, it retrieves web pages in real time as evidence, and generates a direct answer with sources cited sentence by sentence. Among tech circles this experience resonated instantly—search could be about getting an answer, not hunting for links.

Perplexity's core is the marriage of retrieval and generation. It uses a large model to understand the question while fetching relevant pages live, then organizes an answer grounded in those real sources with citations. This "retrieve first, generate with grounding" approach was more reliable than answers from model memory and more direct than traditional search. It landed precisely on the 2022 late-year wave of AI excitement and frustration with search.

Its virality was more than product success; it was a paradigm signal. Traditional search organized information into link lists and left the filtering to users. Perplexity handed filtering, synthesis, and answering to AI—users only need to ask. That shift made "AI search" a standalone category and forced giants like Google and Microsoft to weave AI answers into their search products, permanently changing the competitive dimensions of the industry.

Perplexity was not a simple replacement for the search giants. It was more like tearing a crack in the search ecosystem, proving "answer engines" had commercial space as standalone products, while raising long-running debates about citation, copyright, and traffic distribution. But whatever the disputes, it was the first time the near-natural premise that "search equals link lists" was shaken at scale.

Looking back at Perplexity's launch, it records the starting point of AI's impact on the search industry. By 2026, when users are used to getting cited answers directly in a chat box, that small launch in December 2022 marks the moment the door opened.
