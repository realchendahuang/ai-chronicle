By early 2024, the large-model world had split into two camps: GPT-4 held the performance throne, while Anthropic's Claude, praised for being safe and responsible, was constantly questioned with "but is it a step behind on capability?" For a company that wrote alignment into its mission, that doubt was an irksome burden. Anthropic needed a chance to prove itself.

On March 4, 2024, the chance arrived. The Claude 3 family launched all at once in three tiers: flagship Opus, balanced Sonnet, and lightweight Haiku. All three supported vision input and 200K-token context. The biggest surprise was Opus—it beat GPT-4 on many benchmarks, the first time a Claude model truly broke into the first tier on performance.

Claude 3 changed more than the numbers. It made "tiering" a product philosophy for the first time: one family, divided by capability, speed, and cost, so users could choose by scenario and budget. Haiku was cheap enough for massive calling; Opus was strong enough for flagship work. This "one generation, many tiers" design later became standard across the industry.

For Anthropic, Claude 3 was self-validation. It proved a company that put safety first could still build top-tier models, and that "safety" and "capability" need not be mutually exclusive. After the release, Claude rapidly took hold in the enterprise market, especially in safety- and compliance-sensitive industries, turning Anthropic from an "idealistic challenger" into a serious force.

Looking back, Claude 3's weight is twofold: externally, it won Anthropic respect on performance for the first time; internally, it redefined how model product lines are designed with its tiering. Since then, nearly every flagship launch comes with tiers. In that spring of 2024, GPT-4 was no longer the only choice—and Anthropic's comeback became one of the most memorable stories in AI competition.
