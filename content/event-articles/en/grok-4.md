On July 8, 2025 xAI released Grok 4. For most people the impact wasn't another new model—it was the fact that Musk's model had, for the first time, taken a seat at the same table as GPT, Claude, and Gemini, becoming an official player in reasoning flagships. xAI was no longer "Musk's side project".

Back in 2023, when xAI was founded, Grok's positioning was full of personality—"pursue truth, few limits", styled after its owner. But early Grok genuinely lagged the leaders in capability, and many treated it as a gimmick. What xAI did over the following years was plain: throw enormous compute at the problem, iterate fast, and let time validate the path.

Grok 4 was the fruit of that path. Reasoning, coding, and multimodality jumped, and multiple evals put it in the top tier. Combined with integration into X, it held a distinctive position on real-time information and open data. Around the launch xAI closed a massive funding round and its valuation soared—capital voted in hard currency: Musk's "buy time with compute" play had caught up.

Grok 4's significance went beyond xAI itself. It reshaped the top-tier landscape: what had been three companies trading blows became at least four. One more heavyweight means faster iteration across the industry, more rational pricing, and more choice for developers. It also answered a lingering question—can a latecomer catch the leaders by pouring in compute? This time, the answer leaned toward "yes".

Looking back at Grok 4, the most worth recording is a single fact: the ceiling of competition was raised. When a company willing to spend tens of billions on compute becomes a permanent player, the flagship war stops being a gentle oligopoly. Grok 4 made the game more intense—and more interesting.
