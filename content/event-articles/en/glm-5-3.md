On August 14, 2026, Zhipu released GLM-5.3. The most notable things in the release materials are not the parameters—the base is the same as GLM-5.2—but two others: coding reported up about 50% from the previous generation, and a promise to open weights "within two weeks after safety hardening and evaluation."

On the first: while Kimi K3's 2.8T and Qwen3.8-Max's 2.4T pushed open flagships into a scale race, Zhipu chose a different road—same base, intelligence ceiling raised through aggressive post-training scaling (RL). By Zhipu's account, GLM-5.3 was "widely regarded as the strongest open coding model at the time," taking first place among open models on Terminal Bench 3.0 and Agents' Last Exam (CLI). Read with the vendor-self-reported caveat, but the direction is clear: scale is not the only road; post-training quality can rewrite the intelligence ceiling.

The second deserves more attention. GLM-5.3 ships under a new GLM-5.3 License replacing MIT: institutions with over $10 billion in annual revenue must pass Zhipu's safety review before offering it as an external model service. This is the first time an open model wrote "safety review" into its license terms—reopening the question of what open means. Supporters call it responsible open source; critics worry it sets a precedent for "conditional open." Either way, one fact stands: open is no longer just "weights released" but "released under what conditions."

The "open in two weeks" promise matters too. It turns open-sourcing from an optional post-launch step into part of the release process—release first, evaluate, then open, with the cadence written into the product plan. This continues the rhythm Zhipu showed with the GLM-5 teaser on August 3: tease first, release, iterate fast.

GLM-5.3's meaning is not in any single number. It showed that post-training scaling can rewrite the open flagship's intelligence ceiling outside the scale race; the "open in two weeks" promise and the new license also wrote safety review into the open-source process. For developers, this is a leading open coding base, deployable and fine-tunable in two weeks—but read the license before commercial use; for the industry, open-flagship competition extended from parameter scale to post-training quality, with safety review entering the open-source process. The scale race continues, but this week at least, Zhipu showed another road.
