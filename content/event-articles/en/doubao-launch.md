In August 2023 ByteDance launched an AI chat app: Doubao. In a year when everyone was talking about ChatGPT, Chinese AI assistants were already plentiful, and Doubao looked like just another chatbot. But it carried ByteDance's special playbook: backed by massive traffic from Douyin and other apps, plus a free strategy, Doubao could push a large-model product into millions of ordinary hands in record time. For many people, Doubao was their first free, conversational AI assistant on a phone.

Doubao's technical base was ByteDance's self-developed Skylark model, which later grew into the Doubao model family. ByteDance was patient about AI—from model training to app distribution, it nearly perfected the loop of "self-built model + own traffic + free user acquisition." While other companies agonized over API pricing and enterprise expansion, Doubao had already made consumer scale its moat.

In May 2024 ByteDance formally launched "Doubao LLM," opening the model to the outside with API prices far below industry norms. That launch turned Doubao from an app into a platform: models out, apps in, free strategy continuing to pull users, and low-price APIs grabbing developers. ByteDance's intent was clear—seize scale first, talk profit later.

Doubao's success validated a logic that wins again and again in China: when technical capability is comparable, whoever is closer to users, free, and has traffic wins. This logic kept Doubao at the top of domestic AI apps by user count and made "free plus distribution" the mainstream paradigm of China's consumer AI market.

Looking back, Doubao's launch value lies not in technical breakthrough but in market demonstration. It proved that in China, scale matters more than parameters and distribution matters more than benchmarks. By 2026, when ByteDance has become a major player in global AI through Doubao and the Seed models, the story begins with a free AI assistant that appeared in August 2023.
