In September 2024 one of the most significant names in AI—Fei-Fei Li—announced a new company, World Labs, betting on a then-niche direction: spatial intelligence. While generative AI was busy generating images and videos, Li's choice stood apart: she wanted AI to understand not 2D pixels but 3D worlds.

Li's judgment had deep roots. As the creator of ImageNet, she knew that the heart of visual intelligence was never "recognizing images" but understanding space, objects, and physical rules. As large models learned to express the world in text and images, she believed the next real frontier was AI generating "worlds you can walk into"—with geometry, physics, and interactivity.

World Labs moved fast. It closed a huge funding round within months and shipped its first product by year-end: a model that generates a 3D world from a single sentence, real-time and interactive. In demos users could "enter" the AI-generated world and explore freely, with scenes following basic geometric and physical rules. This wasn't a diagram in a paper; it was something you could experience.

Its meaning went beyond coolness. For film, games, and robot training, "quickly generating credible 3D environments with AI" is genuine infrastructure. For embodied AI, interactive generative worlds mean cheaper training grounds. World Labs turned world models from marketing talk into product and made "spatial intelligence" the focus of a new wave of investment and competition.

Looking back at World Labs' founding, it sounds like the starting gun of a new track. The first half of the generative-AI era competed over who could "make images"; the second half's imagination is about AI "making worlds". Fei-Fei Li put her prestige and judgment behind that imagination—and how far the road goes, autumn 2024 was just the first step.
