In October 2023, at Baidu World, Robin Li announced ERNIE 4.0 with a weighty claim: "comprehensive capability on par with GPT-4". In an era when domestic models were widely seen as chasers, this was the first time a Chinese flagship publicly and directly claimed to match the world's strongest model at that moment.

Six months earlier, ERNIE Bot had debuted as "China's ChatGPT", grabbing first-mover attention while carrying doubts about its strength. For the next six months Baidu iterated on technology while wiring ERNIE into search, maps, Wenku, and cloud storage. By the 4.0 launch, Li demoed complex reasoning and multi-turn dialogue on stage, trying to answer the question "are Chinese models good enough" with actual performance.

ERNIE 4.0's positioning was comprehensive capability gains across understanding, generation, logic, and memory. More importantly, it was positioned as Baidu's AI base—not just releasing a model, but spreading its capability across the entire product line. Search started giving answers, Maps started understanding dialogue, Wenku started creating. A company's AI transformation unfolded with ERNIE at the center.

From an industry view, ERNIE 4.0 formally started the domestic "match GPT-4" race. In the following year, domestic flagships—Qwen, GLM, DeepSeek—all competed against the "international frontier" yardstick. That posture of head-on challenge, whether or not fully delivered, changed the domestic narrative from "find our own way" to "compete directly".

Looking back at ERNIE 4.0's launch, its value was a statement of attitude: Chinese large models would no longer settle for "usable" but dared to publicly challenge the global best. Today domestic models stand on the world stage as a whole, and at the origin of that climb is ERNIE 4.0's declaration of being "on par"—the first trumpet call.
