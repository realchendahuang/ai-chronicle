In June 2023 Zhipu AI open-sourced ChatGLM-6B on GitHub. Six billion parameters was not large by the standards of the day, but it did something right: it let Chinese developers run a decent conversational model on a consumer GPU for the first time. In the summer of 2023, ChatGPT's heat had reached China, but ordinary developers who wanted to "own" a Chinese-speaking model often had to pay expensive API fees or wait for enterprise access—ChatGLM changed that.

ChatGLM's Chinese ability came from its technical route. Backed by the Tsinghua-linked GLM architecture, Zhipu used large amounts of Chinese data in pretraining, giving it a clear edge in Chinese understanding and generation over many English-first models of the era. Combined with a permissive license and detailed Chinese documentation, ChatGLM spread rapidly among developers and universities, becoming a flagship of domestic open-source models.

Its impact was twofold. For developers, ChatGLM offered a base they could freely modify—finetune, deploy, and integrate into business systems, all in their own hands. For the industry, it proved that "open-source Chinese LLM" was a viable road, directly stimulating a wave of domestic open-source models. From ChatGLM to ChatGLM2, ChatGLM3, and then GLM-4, Zhipu ran the open ecosystem and commercial products in parallel.

Of course, early ChatGLM versions objectively lagged the overseas flagships of their time. Its significance was never "strongest" but "good enough and open"—for countless small teams and individual developers in Chinese scenarios, a locally deployable, freely customizable model was worth far more than an unreachable closed flagship.

Looking back, ChatGLM's release reads like the first breaking of ground for open-source Chinese LLMs. Before it, Chinese developers chased overseas models; after it, they had an option they could control. By 2026, when domestic open models can stand shoulder to shoulder with global leaders, ChatGLM is the starting line that let the Chinese community "run first."
