On July 18, 2024 OpenAI released GPT-4o mini. In the shadow of GPT-4o, the launch was low-key, but its impact was badly underestimated: a "mini flagship" at a fraction of the price made "good enough" the industry mainstream. It redefined "what price of model is worth using".

Developers at the time faced a real dilemma: GPT-4-class models were excellent but expensive; cheap models were too weak. Many applications failed not technically but financially—each request ate into the margin. OpenAI clearly saw the pain point, and GPT-4o mini carved a new position between "capability" and "price".

The market response showed the move landed precisely. Developers migrated within days, and high-frequency, high-volume apps benefited most: chat, summarization, classification, customer service—none of these need the most expensive flagship every time. GPT-4o mini let these applications break even for the first time.

Its ripple effects were bigger. Anthropic and Google followed, shipping their own small models or slashing prices. Throughout H2 2024, "small model plus low price" was the theme, and the cost floor for AI applications was systematically lowered. Competition among model vendors extended, for the first time, from "who is stronger" to "who is cheaper".

Looking back, GPT-4o mini's historical value lies not in technical breakthrough but in business logic: AI's spread needs not just the strongest model but affordable options. When "good enough" becomes an accepted product philosophy, more and more applications can launch—that is the most important lesson GPT-4o mini left the industry.
