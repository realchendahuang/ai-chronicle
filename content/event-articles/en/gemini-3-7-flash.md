On August 13, 2026, Google released Gemini 3.7 Flash—just three weeks after 3.6 Flash. That earlier launch was about "three clearly divided Flash models"; this one is a single model, but it carries plenty of signal: Google calls it the most intelligent workhorse model for coding and agents, with DeepSWE up from 49.0% to 65.3%, WebDev Arena Elo from 1538 to 1588, and AutomationBench from 17.0% to 30.4%.

Read with the vendor-self-reported caveat, but the direction is clear: coding and agents. When 3.6 Flash launched, Google was still explaining the flagship's absence with the workhorse framing; three weeks later, the workhorse ran a lap itself. Compressing the Flash iteration cycle to three weeks means Google has accepted a reality: with 3.5 Pro absent and Gemini 4 pretraining just started, Flash is the main weapon.

More direct than benchmarks is price. Through December 31, 2026, 3.7 Flash is 50% off: $0.75 input and $3.75 output per million tokens, reverting to $1.50/$7.50 on January 1, 2027, with 3.6 Flash included in the discount. Yicai's headline said it plainly: "DeepSeek raises prices, Google cuts them." DeepSeek teased big API price changes in August; Google answered with a 50% cut the same week—the price war shifted from "who is cheaper" to "who moves first."

The distribution channels deserve equal attention. 3.7 Flash launched on Google Antigravity, the Gemini API, Gemini Spark, and the Enterprise Agent Platform. Gemini Spark, the agent product for Google AI Pro/Ultra subscribers in 160+ countries, picked up the new model on launch day; the Enterprise Agent Platform covers the corporate side. Personal and enterprise doors updating together shows Google treats Flash as an all-channel workhorse, not a developer experiment.

Three-week iteration, limited-time 50% cut, all-channel rollout—read together, the signal is not "Google released another model" but "Google chose efficiency and price as its way to compete while the flagship is absent." For developers, the workhorse cadence went from "twice a year" to "every three weeks," compressing selection windows; for the industry, Flash became Google's main weapon, spreading the price war to the workhorse tier. As for when 3.5 Pro arrives, the release page has no answer—but this week, Google answered "what do we do without a flagship" with action.
