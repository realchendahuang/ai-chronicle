In May 2023 NVIDIA's market value broke $1 trillion intraday after an earnings report, making it the first chip company ever to reach that height. To many ordinary users the name still meant "graphics cards"; to the AI industry, though, this moment was a long time coming—when the whole world is rushing to buy GPUs to train large models, the maker of those GPUs becomes the only "shovel seller" in the gold rush.

NVIDIA's eruption was not overnight. Years of accumulation in the data-center business, the lock-in of the CUDA ecosystem, and a lead in compute in each new generation all paved the way. But the true trigger was the generative-AI wave ignited by ChatGPT in late 2022. Training and serving large models demands GPUs at a scale supply could not meet, and NVIDIA's chips became a scarce strategic commodity.

The trillion-dollar valuation meant the capital market had finished repricing a shift: AI was no longer just a software story, but a full-chain industry of chips, data centers, energy, and power. GPUs were redefined from "gaming parts" to "the infrastructure of the AI era," like oil for the industrial age. That cognitive shift shaped the next two years of industry narrative—the compute supply chain became a focus of geopolitical and capital competition, and every country treated chips as a strategic resource.

Of course, the trillion-dollar mark also opened new questions. Concerns about concentration and dependence, the squeeze on smaller developers from GPU shortages, and the interest structure between the "shovel seller" and the "miners" all became ongoing industry topics. NVIDIA's rise proved the value of AI compute while also making the "compute divide" a new source of inequality.

Looking back at the moment NVIDIA passed a trillion dollars, it stands as a watershed: before it, AI talked about algorithms and models; after it, AI started talking about chips, power, and supply chains. When data centers in 2026 are still fighting over GPUs and electricity, that trillion-dollar mark in May 2023 is where it all began.
