On September 7, 2023 Tencent officially released its Hunyuan large model. The timing was not early by domestic standards—Baidu, Alibaba, and Huawei had all shown their own models in the first half of the year, and the market had been labeling Tencent a laggard. But Hunyuan's launch completed a fact: from that day, every Chinese internet giant stood at the self-developed model table.

Tencent's lateness owed less to neglect than to caution. As a company that puts product experience first, it was more conservative about which scenarios a model should plug into and how data would be handled. Mid-year it reorganized its AI team and completed Hunyuan's registration; the September launch was the natural result. Hunyuan started from text capability with code and image generation, and explicitly took an "industry-practical" positioning.

After launch, Hunyuan quickly seeped into Tencent's vast product matrix. Smart suggestions in WeChat input, creation assistance in Tencent Docs, APIs on Tencent Cloud—all began running on Hunyuan. Unlike models that "release first, find scenarios later", Hunyuan was almost born inside Tencent's business muscle. This scenario-driven route is the defining trait of Tencent-style AI.

Hunyuan's significance went further. It evolved into a family: Hunyuan painting, video, speech, and 3D. Its 2024 video model briefly led Chinese video generation, and Hy ASR 3.0 in 2026 kept advancing speech recognition. To understand any of Tencent's generative-AI moves in recent years, the Hunyuan large model is the unavoidable starting point.

Looking back at the launch, it marked the closing of an era: China's internet giants had finally all obtained their own model bases. Tencent filled its piece in a "late but pragmatic" way and found its own rhythm in multimodal and industrial application. When a company's AI goes from bought to self-built, the competitive landscape is never quite the same.
