In March 2024 a Chinese AI assistant named Kimi suddenly swept the internet. Its selling point was simple and direct: it could read up to two million characters in one go. What does that mean? A book of several hundred thousand characters, several hundred-page PDFs, even an entire prospectus—all of it could be handed over at once, read completely, and answered. After ChatGPT, "an AI that can read long documents" became a nationwide topic for the first time.

Kimi's rise was no accident. Moonshot had emphasized long text since Kimi's October 2023 debut, and after the Spring Festival it pushed the window to two million characters and opened it to all users. The product made high-frequency needs—reading PDFs, papers, and organizing materials—remarkably smooth: upload, ask, get an answer with sources. The "long text plus free access" combination hit the real pain point of domestic AI users at that moment.

Its spread was also telling. No advertising blitz—just users sharing spontaneously: someone feeding it a prospectus, someone analyzing financial reports, someone processing a long memo. In an era still educating people on "what AI can do", Kimi used the concrete scenario of reading long documents to make the usefulness of large models tangible to ordinary users. It was the first time a domestic AI app entered the public eye at such scale.

Kimi's breakout triggered a chain reaction across the domestic field. Vendors rushed to follow: some pushed windows past a million, others put "ultra-long documents" in their taglines. Moonshot jumped to the top of the "AI 六小龙" in mindshare, and long context went from a special feature to a default capability. The consumer-side landscape was reshuffled by this breakout.

Looking back at Kimi's long-context moment, its value was a successful product education: showing the public that AI isn't just chat—it can genuinely help process massive amounts of information. Today million-character context is standard among domestic flagships, but that spring-2024 assistant that "could read two million characters" is where it all began.
