In December 2024 Amazon unveiled the Nova foundation models at re:Invent. For anyone following cloud computing, this was a long-awaited piece of the puzzle—while Microsoft had OpenAI, Google had Gemini, and even Alibaba had Qianwen, Amazon as the world's largest cloud vendor had long relied on partners like Anthropic for model capability. Nova marked Amazon finally filling the in-house model gap.

Nova's positioning was very Amazon: pragmatic, price-performance focused. It covers text understanding, creative generation, multimodal, and video generation, but the message was not "strongest" but "efficient"—keeping quality while pushing inference costs into a reasonable range. The models are offered through Bedrock, letting enterprise users choose between in-house Nova and external models like Anthropic's Claude as needed.

This "self-built plus partner" dual track is the core of Amazon's model strategy. On one hand, in-house Nova means Amazon is not fully at others' mercy on model pricing and capability; on the other, deep cooperation with Anthropic (including major investment and compute partnership) keeps frontier models in the AWS ecosystem. Walking on two legs holds customers steady and preserves choice.

Nova's significance reaches beyond one company. It again proved that large models have moved from an "optional AI capability" to "standard cloud infrastructure"—like storage and compute, without your own model, a cloud platform lacks part of its foundation. AWS's entry pushed the cloud-vendor in-house race into its final catch-up phase.

Looking back at Nova's release, the most interesting parts are timing and posture. No rush to be first, no claim of being strongest, but a steady landing exactly when enterprises needed price-performance models. Amazon used Nova to complete a low-key yet important gap-fill—from now on, every enterprise customer in the AWS ecosystem can find the most suitable model between "self-built" and "partner."
