In January 2024 Zhipu released GLM-4. Among domestic models, Zhipu's identity is distinctive: born from a Tsinghua team, starting with open-source ChatGLM, it runs a dual-engine path of academic reputation and commercial services. GLM-4 was the first true flagship upgrade on that road.

Unlike startups building from zero, Zhipu's technical foundation had long been accumulating in the open-source community. ChatGLM enjoyed a large developer base, so GLM-4 arrived with built-in community expectation. The launch showcased big gains in Chinese-language ability and tool calling, with APIs opened at the same time. In the domestic first tier, Zhipu is one of the few players that can serve up both open weights and commercial services.

GLM-4's positioning was clear: Chinese scenarios first, tool calling and agent capability in parallel. This also reflected the new focus of domestic model competition in early 2024—no longer "who scores higher" but "who can get work done". Around the model, Zhipu built agent, coding, and industry-solution layouts, trying to sink model capability into landable commercial products.

The GLM line's value lies in diversifying the domestic open-source ecosystem. DeepSeek is known for extreme cost-performance, Qwen rides Alibaba's ecosystem, and GLM represents the academic-born, community-driven road. Together they complement each other, offering different cross-sections of domestic open models. For developers, more roads mean more choices.

Looking back at GLM-4, it marked Zhipu's shift from "an open-source project people remembered" to "a flagship player people recognized". Since then GLM kept iterating, and even the GLM-5 teaser of 2026 drew attention. Zhipu proved with each release that academic rigor and commercial drive can coexist—and that made domestic model competition all the more vivid.
