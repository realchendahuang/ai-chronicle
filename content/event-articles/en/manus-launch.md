On March 6, 2025 a Chinese AI product named Manus flooded the internet. Its self-introduction was simple: a general AI agent that completes "whole tasks" for you—filtering résumés, writing analysis reports, researching stocks, organizing materials. In demo videos Manus did not chat with you turn by turn; it acted like a real person, opening a browser, visiting pages, clicking, organizing results, and delivering a finished product.

Manus adopted the era's favorite launch format: the invite-code economy. There was no open registration; you needed a code to use it. Social platforms filled with people begging for codes, and secondary markets sold them for thousands of yuan, reaching "a code is harder to get than a ticket" proportions. Servers overloaded under the flood, and the company had to throttle. An AI product triggering such a frenzy had almost no precedent.

Technically, Manus belonged to the fast-maturing "autonomous agent" route: a large model handled understanding and planning, a tool-calling framework let it operate browsers and software, and async execution let tasks run long in the background. It was not an original breakthrough in base models but a system combining existing models, tools, and orchestration to deliver complete tasks—and this kind of engineering integration was itself the most valuable capability of the agent era.

The discussion around Manus quickly shifted from "is it useful" to "is it real." People tested it, some questioned the selective demos, others pointed out it still errs on complex tasks. None of that debate obscured one fact: it made "handing a whole task to AI" a reality the public could see with their own eyes. 2025 has been called the "year of agents," and Manus was the match that lit that narrative.

After the hype passed, neither Manus's team nor the industry stopped. Chinese AI startups refocused on agents, and giants made agents a strategic priority. Later general-agent products—from Manus itself to OpenAI's agents—have all followed the road Manus opened in spring 2025. A product's heat fades, but the question it defined—how AI takes over complete tasks—became the industry's mainline for the following years.

Looking back at March 2025, Manus's greatest contribution was turning "autonomous agents" from a technical term into a collective imagination. It may not have been the technically strongest, but it was the first to let everyone see that "AI can finish a task by itself." When every later agent news story describes itself "like Manus," that spring's flood had quietly written itself into AI history.
