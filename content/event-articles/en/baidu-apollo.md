In the spring of 2017, Waymo was the undisputed leader on the self-driving track, running a closed, in-house play: technology kept under lock and key, test cars quietly piling up miles on public roads. Baidu held cards too—it had started early on autonomous driving—but going it alone against an American giant looked like a losing game. So Baidu changed tactics: stop hiding the technology and open it up.

On April 19, 2017, Baidu unveiled the Apollo program, opening core modules for perception, localization, and planning at once, complete with HD maps and simulation tools. The logic was simple: rather than building in secret, share the chassis and let automakers and developers around the world build on your framework, using the scale of an ecosystem to counter the depth of a single rival. The initial partner list already included several automakers and suppliers.

Apollo grew at a startling pace. The open platform drew developers from everywhere and quickly became one of the world's largest open self-driving ecosystems. For automakers wanting to enter self-driving without full-stack capability, Apollo offered a shortcut. China's voice on this track was, to a large extent, established from Apollo onward.

Of course, openness is not a magic cure. The hardest part of self-driving was never the framework but safety validation and massive real-world data; Apollo also went through route adjustments and commercial ups and downs. But it validated an important industry idea: when the technology gap is not insurmountable, ecosystem competition can rewrite the winner-take-all script.

Looking back, Apollo's significance lies in offering the self-driving industry a path hypothesis different from Waymo's—closed versus open, and no one knew which would reach the destination. Years later, when robotaxis began landing in Chinese cities and assisted driving matured across automakers, people looking back at that 2017 launch would remember it as the moment China's self-driving first showed its cards to the world with an open posture.
