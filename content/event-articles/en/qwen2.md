By mid-2024, the open-source large-model world was bustling: Llama 3 had 8B and 70B, Mistral had its 7B flagship, and everyone was jockeying for position on the open track. But a closer look showed most of these were "single-point operations"—one star size at a time, forcing developers to switch and re-adapt between different models.

Alibaba's Qwen team broke that pattern in June 2024. The Qwen2 release open-sourced five sizes at once: 0.5B, 1.5B, 7B, 57B-A14B, and 72B, with a unified architecture and ecosystem. From phone-sized models to server-grade flagships, everything was given in a single delivery. The 7B and 72B beat open rivals at their sizes on many benchmarks, and 32K context covered most scenarios.

The experience of "serialization" was revolutionary. Developers no longer had to adapt different sizes to different models—the same Qwen2 family ran small on edge devices and handled heavy lifting with the big ones, with minimal migration cost. Qwen2 quickly became one of the most-used open bases globally, with Hugging Face downloads climbing fast.

Of course, serialization had its price. With more versions, the community faced fragmentation, and users had to spend more effort choosing. But that did not diminish Qwen2's significance: it showed the world for the first time that "open models should be a whole family, not a few isolated points." This play was later imitated by countless vendors.

Looking back, Qwen2 was a key step on Chinese open models' road to global leadership. With one release it covered everything from toys to production, giving "Chinese open source" real heft for the first time. When Qwen continued to rule open leaderboards in later versions, people would remember that it all began, in June 2024, with this "family bundle."
