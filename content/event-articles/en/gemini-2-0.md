By late 2024 a new keyword dominated the large-model industry: agent. OpenAI had its self-reasoning o-series, Anthropic had computer use that operated a machine, and everyone said "the next era is models doing work for people." For Google, as a search giant, this narrative was both tempting and dangerous—it had to prove it was not just "best at chat" but could also "get things done."

On December 11, 2024, Google gave its answer: Gemini 2.0 Flash. This version natively supported image, video, and audio output, with built-in tool use and multimodal reasoning. But the centerpiece of the launch was the demos—Project Mariner was an agent that operated a browser like a human, Deep Research could autonomously search and compile reports, and there was even an agent that played games. The core message of the entire event: the information age is moving into the agentic age.

Gemini 2.0's strategic posture was unmistakable. Google did not treat agents as an add-on feature but wrote them into the flagship model's roadmap. The signal: future models are not for answering questions but for completing tasks. This "agent-first" declaration shifted the product narrative across the industry.

Of course, impressive demos and difficult deployment always walk together. Browser agents still make mistakes on complex pages, and autonomous long-horizon execution is far from stable. Gemini 2.0 was more a declaration of direction than a delivery of the end state. But the declaration itself carried weight.

Looking back, Gemini 2.0 was Google's proactive turn in the agent era. It did not invent the agent, but it turned "models doing work for people" from a vague vision into black and white on a giant's roadmap. Since then, nearly every major vendor's launch has revolved around the word "agent"—and Google chose that winter of 2024 to be the first to say it out loud.
