summaryEn: Warren McCulloch and Walter Pitts published "A Logical Calculus of the Ideas Immanent in Nervous Activity," showing that simple artificial neurons could perform logical operations. The paper gave neural networks and AI their mathematical foundation.
background: 二战期间，数理逻辑、神经科学与计算理论开始交汇。麦克洛克是神经生理学家，皮茨是天才少年逻辑学家，两人在芝加哥相遇后决定用数学描述神经元。
backgroundEn: During World War II, mathematical logic, neuroscience, and computing theory began to converge. McCulloch was a neurophysiologist, Pitts a prodigy logician; they met in Chicago and set out to describe neurons mathematically.
whatHappened: 1943 年两人发表论文，把神经元建模为带阈值的二值开关：输入经过加权求和，超过阈值就输出 1，否则输出 0。他们证明这种网络的组合可以模拟与、或、非等逻辑运算，等同于一台有限状态机。
whatHappenedEn: 'In 1943 the two published a paper modeling neurons as binary switches with thresholds: weighted inputs sum up and fire a 1 if above threshold, else 0. They showed networks of such units could simulate AND, OR, NOT, and act as finite-state machines.'
whyImportant: 它第一次把「思考」还原为可计算的逻辑演算，直接启发了感知机与后来的深度学习。几乎今天所有神经网络，都能追溯到这篇论文的框架。
whyImportantEn: It was the first attempt to reduce "thinking" to computable logical calculus, directly inspiring the perceptron and later deep learning. Nearly every neural network today can trace back to this paper's framework.
