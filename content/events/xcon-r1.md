summaryEn: Carnegie Mellon built XCON (originally R1) for DEC, using thousands of rules to automatically configure VAX computer orders. Widely considered the first successful commercial expert system, it proved AI's business value in the enterprise.
background: 专家系统在 1970 年代积累了一批学术原型（如 MYCIN），但一直停留在实验室。1980 年代初，DEC 的 VAX 配置订单极其复杂，错误频出，急需自动化。
backgroundEn: Expert systems had accumulated academic prototypes like MYCIN in the 1970s but stayed in labs. In the early 1980s DEC's VAX configuration orders were extremely complex and error-prone, demanding automation.
whatHappened: 卡内基梅隆的约翰·麦克德莫特为 DEC 编写 R1，后改名 XCON。系统包含数千条配置规则，输入订单规格后能自动生成正确的组件清单与布线方案，准确率超过人工。DEC 将其投入生产，年节省数百万美元。
whatHappenedEn: John McDermott at Carnegie Mellon wrote R1 for DEC, later renamed XCON. With thousands of configuration rules, the system took order specs and automatically generated correct component lists and wiring plans, outperforming humans in accuracy. DEC put it into production, saving millions yearly.
whyImportant: XCON 证明了规则系统可以产生可观的经济回报，点燃了 1980 年代的专家系统产业热潮，也埋下了后来知识工程兴衰的伏笔。
whyImportantEn: XCON proved rule systems could yield real economic returns, igniting the 1980s expert-system boom and foreshadowing the rise and fall of the knowledge-engineering era.
